The Challenge

Creating an offline meeting opportunity for young adults who feel isolated in a world shaped by digital self-presentation — encouraging genuine, low-pressure encounters and authentic human connection.

Original brief slide from the design thinking session
Research

We started by mapping the research landscape: academic studies on social media use and loneliness, news coverage, and statistics from public health reports. Key findings confirmed that the problem disproportionately affects young adults and is strongly linked to passive online consumption.

Research sources: academic papers and news articles on loneliness among young adults
01
Transition problem
Young adults struggle to move from digital interaction to real-life connection — the threshold for first contact is too high.
02
Offline preference
Users feel shaped and overwhelmed by the digital world — they actively want offline spaces to be themselves without performance pressure.
03
Shared context
Shared activities — especially meals — reduce social anxiety by giving people a built-in focus that takes the pressure off direct conversation.
04
Safety & trust
For the experience to work, participants need to feel safe — clear social norms, structured environments, and light facilitation are essential.
Target Group
User Personas

We developed three detailed personas to ground our design decisions in real human stories.

Ideation

Our ideation phase was deliberately messy and divergent. Working in groups with Post-Its and sketches, we explored dozens of directions — from digital matchmaking apps to physical community boards. The ideas were rated, clustered, and refined across several rounds.

The Solution

Meat & Eat is a recurring social dinner format for young adults (18–26), designed to encourage real-life connection through shared Friday evening dinners at different restaurants across Munich. Participants sign up via Instagram and receive the location shortly before the event, creating a relaxed and slightly unexpected social experience.

Key Features
Event team structure — Awareness Team and Undercover Moderators working in the background
Problem-solution mapping for shyness, group clustering, orientation and inappropriate behaviour
01
Undercover Moderators
One trained moderator per table stays anonymous — sparking conversations, including shy guests, and monitoring the atmosphere without creating performance pressure.
02
Awareness Team
A small organisational team handles registration, moderator briefing, and on-site check-in — ensuring smooth flow and a point of contact for any issues.
03
Mixed Seating
Seats are assigned randomly to prevent existing friend groups from clustering, encouraging genuine new connections across the whole table.
04
Conversation Starters
Topic cards at each table give guests a natural way to break the ice — especially helpful for introverted or shy participants in the first 30 minutes.
